# Journal voice

How the travel guides should sound. The reference is the Kyoto guide (`_posts/2023-05-10-kyoto.html`),
the first one that went online: one person who travels with a designer's eye, telling a friend what
was worth it and how to do it well. Folders starting with `_` are not published by Jekyll.

## What makes Kyoto work

- **A real person is talking.** First person, warm, a bit of humor at my own expense:
  "I'm still not over it", "Don't judge", "No guarantees though", "If I remember correctly".
- **Honest opinions.** It says when something disappointed and why: the bamboo forest "wasn't my
  personal favorite stop… it was already quite busy and the forest was shorter than I thought".
- **A small story, then a tip.** Each entry has a moment from the trip (the Maiko who posed for a
  photo, arriving at Hōkan-ji at 8am) and something the reader can act on: when to go, what to
  order, how long the wait is, where to buy the cheaper ticket. Tips can be marked `<strong>Tip:</strong>`.
- **Context in a sentence.** A bit of history or background where it helps (Kinkaku-ji was built as a
  shogun's retirement villa), never a Wikipedia paragraph.
- **The designer's eye.** Lemon illustrations on a menu, a café where you sit on steps, a Starbucks
  with tatami mats. Materials, interiors, branding and light are what make these guides different.
- **Talks to the reader.** "If you share my love for…", "Can't make it to Kyoto anytime soon?"

## Rules for every post

- **Looking back.** Past tense for what I did ("I arrived around 8am"), present tense only for what is
  still true of the place ("the train takes about 25 minutes"). Name the trip once in the intro,
  never "today", "this week" or "right now".
- **Stories are the source, not the text.** Keep the observation and the feeling, rewrite the words.
  "Ordered the entire menu. 10/10 recommend." becomes a sentence a reader learns something from.
  Never invent memories: if the stories don't say it, add a general, checkable fact instead, or leave it out.
- **Every entry is useful.** Two to five sentences. At least one thing a reader can act on.
- **No repetition across posts.** Every intro opens differently and is specific to the place. No
  "This guide collects…", "First time in…!", "In [month] I spent…" formulas, and no sentence pattern
  used twice in a row. Before publishing, search the other posts for the new intro's phrases.
- **Keep the brochure words rare.** Kyoto allows itself a "must-visit" or "breathtaking" now and then;
  that's fine, but not more than once or twice per post, and never "hidden gem" or "10/10".

## Structure

- **Intro (description):** two paragraphs. The first says what this place meant to me and when I was
  there. The second says what the reader will find, in words specific to this place.
- **At a glance:** facts only. Labels in this order: Where I stayed, Getting there, Getting around,
  How long, Areas, Highlights, Favorite food, Good to know.
- **Section descriptions:** a few sentences of orientation and how to plan the day.

## Spelling and style

- American English, as in Kyoto: favorite, color, neighborhood, flavor, traveled.
- Numbers: one to ten in words, 11 and up in digits; times as 8am, 7:30pm.
- Place names in their local spelling with accents (Sóller, Sensō-ji, Prückel).
- Facts that didn't come from the stories (prices, opening times, closures) are checked before publishing.
